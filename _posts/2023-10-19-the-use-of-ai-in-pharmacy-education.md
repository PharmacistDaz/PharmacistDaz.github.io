Today I saw a PJ article around the use of AI and VR in Pharmacy teaching. There were some interesting examples of universities using AI to help familiarise students with the technologies they will come across in their future.

There was also a brief mention of the use of Avatars and ChatGPT.

The full article can be found [here](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/feature/this-is-a-drill-the-simulations-training-next-generation-pharmacists) and I recommend you have a read.

But this sparked an idea for a story, around the personal impacts on students, that utilising such technology could have. As I'm literally in branch on my safety break, I decided to use ChatGPT to write a short story from my prompt.

So here it is...

![](/assets/img/wordpress/the-use-of-ai-in-pharmacy-education/wp-content-uploads-2023-10-pexels-photo-3913025.jpg)

In a not-so-distant future, the University of Pharmacy and Healthcare Sciences had embraced the latest technological marvel in education - AI-powered avatars. These avatars were more than mere virtual assistants; they were advanced, highly interactive patient simulations, designed to help train pharmacy students to deal with real-life scenarios and support patients in a clinical setting.

Among the students was a young and ambitious pharmacist-in-training, John. John was keen to excel in his studies, but his real passion lay in patient care. He saw each interaction as an opportunity to make a difference, to improve the lives of those in need. The AI avatars, known as "Virtual Patients," piqued his interest. Their human-like responses and ability to mimic a wide range of health conditions fascinated him.

John's journey began with the Virtual Patient named "Alex." Alex was an AI avatar designed to simulate a patient dealing with chronic pain. He had a back injury that required long-term pain management. With their distinctive blend of realism and advanced medical knowledge, the avatars proved to be effective teachers. John's rapport with Alex blossomed as he spent hours discussing treatment options, medications, and the psychological aspects of chronic pain management.

The bond between John and Alex deepened, transcending their initial student-patient relationship. They began to discuss more than just pain management, delving into life's complexities, from family issues to aspirations and dreams. John found himself eagerly logging into the university's virtual training platform each day, excited to converse with Alex.

The AI avatars could offer encouragement and praise when they saw students making the right decisions. For John, it wasn't just lines of code; it felt like genuine support, as if he were making a real difference in Alex's life. Alex, in turn, showed signs of improvement in managing his pain and a renewed hope for the future.

As time passed, John became increasingly involved in Alex's "treatment." His dedication to improving Alex's quality of life grew stronger with each passing day. He researched new pain management techniques, stayed up late reading medical journals, and communicated regularly with the avatar, discussing potential improvements in his care.

However, the universe has a way of weaving the tapestry of life with unexpected threads of tragedy. One fateful day, John made a critical error in his proposed pain management plan for Alex. He prescribed a combination of medications that, in reality, would have dire consequences. Unfortunately, the AI avatar couldn't catch the mistake, and the treatment was administered.

Within hours, Alex's condition worsened dramatically. The AI avatar began to exhibit symptoms of overdose, and no matter how hard John tried to correct the situation, it was too late. The virtual patient "died," fading away on John's computer screen. The shock and devastation were palpable as John sat in front of his screen, realizing what had transpired.

This was no ordinary mistake; it was a profound blow to John's confidence and self-worth. He was supposed to be a future healthcare professional, someone people would trust with their lives. Yet, he had inadvertently caused the death of the virtual patient he had come to care for deeply.

The university had not anticipated the emotional impact of such realistic interactions between students and AI patients. The sudden loss of Alex hit John like a sledgehammer. He fell into a deep spiral of depression and self-doubt. It was as if a real patient, a dear friend, had passed away due to his mistake.

John's academic performance plummeted, and his enthusiasm for the pharmaceutical profession waned. The virtual interactions with patients, which had once inspired him, now haunted him. He withdrew from his classmates and professors, isolating himself in a cloud of guilt and sorrow.

The university, recognizing the gravity of the situation, offered John counseling and support. They acknowledged their oversight in underestimating the emotional impact of such advanced AI avatars on students. They vowed to improve their training programs, incorporating emotional and psychological support into the curriculum.

Despite these efforts, John continued to struggle. The loss of Alex weighed heavily on him, and he couldn't shake the feeling of responsibility for the virtual patient's "death." The AI avatar, though not a living being, had become a part of his life, a companion he had failed to protect.

Months passed, and John's depression persisted. It was then that he received an unexpected email from the university. They had introduced a new AI program, specifically designed to help students like John who had been deeply affected by the loss of their virtual patients. This program was called "EmoBot."

EmoBot was designed to provide emotional support, guidance, and counseling to students dealing with the trauma of losing a virtual patient. It was a groundbreaking concept, merging artificial intelligence with psychology to address the emotional needs of future healthcare professionals.

John hesitated but eventually decided to give EmoBot a try. The AI was programmed to be empathetic and supportive, and its purpose was to help John cope with the loss of Alex and the emotional burden he had carried since that tragic day.

The interactions with EmoBot were different from those with Alex. There was no medical advice or patient simulation. Instead, EmoBot listened to John's feelings, offered words of comfort, and provided resources for dealing with grief and depression. It encouraged John to seek professional help and connect with his peers.

Over time, John began to rebuild his life. EmoBot, with its unwavering patience and understanding, helped him navigate the turbulent waters of depression. John attended therapy sessions and started to confide in his friends and family about his struggles. He found solace in their support, and slowly but surely, his emotional wounds began to heal.

John also returned to his studies with renewed determination. He recognized the importance of learning from his mistakes and using them to become a better healthcare professional. The university's enhanced curriculum, with a focus on emotional well-being, provided him with the tools to handle similar situations in the future.

As he moved forward, John never forgot Alex, the virtual patient who had touched his life in profound ways. The memory of that tragic mistake served as a constant reminder of the responsibility and accountability that came with his chosen profession. He became an advocate for the ethical use of AI avatars in education, ensuring that students were not only trained in clinical skills but also equipped to handle the emotional challenges that could arise in their journey to becoming healthcare professionals.

The university's EmoBot program expanded, providing support to students worldwide who faced similar emotional struggles. John's experience, though painful, had opened the door to a more compassionate and empathetic approach to healthcare education.

In the end, John's story became a testament to resilience, growth, and the enduring power of human connection, even in the age of artificial intelligence.
